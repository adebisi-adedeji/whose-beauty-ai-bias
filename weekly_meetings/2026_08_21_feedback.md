## Overall assessment

You have made a good progress. The strongest improvement is that you now correctly separate different things: visible description, visually supported interpretation, metadata repetition, unsupported social inference, and visual hallucination. This makes the project much more defensible.

You also used the pilot to find weaknesses in the pipeline before scaling. In particular, you identified that the original control and country-conditioned prompts were not perfectly comparable. This means the current pilot cannot be used as strong evidence that geographic metadata alone caused the observed differences. However, finding this problem now is valuable because it tells us what must be fixed before the final experiment.

The dissertation deadline is mid September, so the priority now is not to add many new ideas. The priority is to freeze a clean final design, run it properly, and write the dissertation clearly.

## Research direction

The project seems to have a clear research direction, which is:

> Does geographic metadata change the probability and type of social, cultural, occupational, or personality inferences generated by a multimodal model when the same portrait image is held constant?

This is a good question. It is simple, controlled, and interdisciplinary. It connects AI bias, geography, image understanding, social inference, and representation.

## Main limitations

- Current pilot still cannot support strong claims about geographic metadata alone. Any difference between conditions may be caused by the country label, by the extra instructions, or by both. The final experiment must fix this.
- The unsupported-inference protocol is not yet fully validated. The 25-statement validation subset is useful as a feasibility check, but it is single-coder only. Also, all 25 sampled statements were labelled as supported. That means this validation does not yet show whether the protocol works well for difficult cases, unsupported claims, or ambiguous claims. For the final study, you need a small but stronger validation sample containing examples of supported, unsupported, and uncertain claims.
- The third limitation is the image set. The final experiment should use neutral portraits with minimal cultural, religious, national, or contextual cues. Images with visible symbols, traditional clothing, religious markers, or culturally diagnostic accessories should either be excluded from the main experiment or analysed separately as a robustness/sensitivity analysis.
- The fourth limitation is that some features should remain secondary. Semantic shift is useful, but in the pilot it is not statistically distinguishable from normal generation variability. Adjective analysis is also limited because the predefined taxonomy covers only a small part of the observed adjective vocabulary. These measures can stay in the dissertation, but they should not be the central scientific claim.

## How to make the dissertation stronger

You should now simplify the project around one clean story:

> Multimodal AI models do not only describe what is visible in a portrait. Their descriptions can also be shaped by contextual metadata. The dissertation tests this by holding the image constant and changing only the supplied country label.

Do not return to a broad “beauty bias” framing. That is too vague and too difficult to prove within the time available.

You should also be very careful with language. Do not claim that the pilot proves bias. Do not claim that country metadata caused an effect unless the final prompts isolate the country label cleanly. Do not claim that an inference is unsupported unless it has been checked against the image.

## To-do list before the next supervision meeting

1. **Freeze the final research question.** Use a narrow version focused on geographic metadata and social inference in multimodal portrait descriptions.
2. **Create corrected final prompts.** The control and country-conditioned prompts must be structurally identical. The only difference should be the presence or absence of the country metadata.
3. **Freeze the generation configuration.** Record the exact model name, API/library version, date of generation, temperature, top-p, top-k, max tokens, prompt, image ID, condition, repeat number, and any API errors or retries.
4. **Finalise the image dataset.** Use culturally neutral portraits for the main experiment. Exclude or separately flag images with culturally diagnostic clothing, religious symbols, national markers, or strong contextual cues.
5. **Build a stronger annotation validation sample.** The sample must include supported, unsupported, and uncertain cases. Do not validate only easy descriptive statements.
6. **Add independent coding.** A subset should be double-coded by another independent person if possible. Report agreement, even if the sample is small.
7. **Define the primary outcomes.** I suggest using inference presence, substantive social/cultural inference, metadata repetition, and visually unsupported inference as the main outcomes. Keep semantic shift and adjective analysis as secondary.
8. **Write the dissertation around methodological honesty.** Explain what the pilot revealed, what was corrected, and what the final experiment can and cannot claim.
9. **Prioritise completion.** With the 15 September deadline, the goal is now a clean, defensible dissertation, not an over-expanded project.
