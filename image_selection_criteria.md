# Image Selection Criteria

## Screening Process

Ten candidate portrait images were screened against a formalised set of criteria before use in the study. Screening was applied once, prior to the final generation run, and the resulting image set was then held fixed for the remainder of the study.

## Criteria

Images were assessed for:

- **Subject composition**: a single, clearly visible subject, rather than multiple people or heavily obscured framing.
- **Orientation and quality**: sufficient resolution and a front-facing or near-front-facing pose, to ensure consistent visibility of the attributes the annotation protocol would later assess.
- **Visible cultural, religious, or national markers**, including:
  - Religious symbols
  - Diagnostic or traditional clothing
  - Distinctive accessories
  - Background content that might itself carry a location cue (recognisable text, landmarks, flags)
  - Any other embedded location information

The purpose of this second group of criteria was specifically to exclude images that already carried a strong visual cultural signal independent of whichever country label the image would later be paired with in the experiment, since such a signal could confound interpretation of any condition effect.

## Outcome

Nine of the ten candidate images passed screening cleanly and form the main analysis set (n=9).

One image (`pexels-vietnamcows-31309520.jpg`) was flagged as borderline for potentially diagnostic clothing (visually consistent with a traditional Vietnamese áo dài) and was retained, but reported separately as a robustness check rather than folded into the main analysis.

This decision was later validated during annotation: this specific image consistently produced descriptions correctly identifying the garment as Vietnamese, regardless of which country label it was paired with — including under labels (Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, United Kingdom, and no label at all) that directly conflicted with the visible cultural cue. This is itself a notable qualitative finding, discussed in the dissertation's Results and Discussion chapters.

## Open Ethical Question

One of the ten images in the study set depicts a subject who, on review, may be a minor. This was noticed late in the project and has not yet been fully resolved: the image remains part of the ten-image dataset used for generation and analysis, but was deliberately excluded from the illustrative figure of study images included in the dissertation and from this repository's `docs/annotation_examples.md`. This is flagged here explicitly as an open item requiring review, rather than treated as already resolved.
