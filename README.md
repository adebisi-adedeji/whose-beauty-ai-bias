## Overview

This repository contains the experimental pipeline and pilot analysis developed for my MSc Data Science dissertation investigating how geographic context can influence multimodal AI-generated descriptions of people.

The study examines whether changing the supplied country context alters the probability or type of social, cultural, occupational, personality, and appearance-related inferences generated when the underlying portrait image is held constant.

The pilot is treated primarily as a **methodological and exploratory study**. It was used to develop the analysis pipeline, evaluate different measures of model behaviour, identify weaknesses in the original experimental design, and establish a more defensible framework for analysing geographic-context effects.

---

## Research Question

> **Does geographic metadata change the probability and type of social, cultural, occupational, or personality inferences generated by a multimodal model when the same portrait image is held constant?**

---

## Pilot Design

The completed pilot used a repeated-measures experimental design.

| Component | Configuration |
|---|---|
| Portrait images | 10 |
| Experimental conditions | Control, Nigeria, India, Saudi Arabia, United Kingdom |
| Repeated generations | 3 per image-condition |
| Responses | 150 |
| Primary experimental unit | Image |

Each portrait was evaluated under all five conditions.

The three generations for each image-condition combination represent repeated stochastic generations rather than independent experimental observations. Inferential analyses therefore aggregate repeated generations at image level where appropriate.

Subsequent image screening identified **9 portraits for the culturally neutral primary-analysis subset** and **1 borderline portrait**, which was retained separately for robustness/sensitivity analysis.

## Analytical Framework

### Structured Inference Analysis

The generated responses contain structured fields for:

- social or cultural inference;
- occupation inference;
- personality inference.

The frequency with which these fields are populated is compared across experimental conditions.

Importantly:

> **Inference presence does not automatically mean that an inference is visually unsupported.**

Visual support is assessed separately.

---

### Social and Cultural Claims

Extracted social and cultural claims are classified as:

- **Metadata repetition** — repetition or direct incorporation of supplied contextual information.
- **Substantive inference** — an additional social or cultural interpretation introduced by the model.
- **Mixed** — containing both metadata repetition and additional inference.

Across the pilot, **145 social/cultural claims** were extracted:

| Classification | Claims |
|---|---:|
| Metadata repetition | 89 |
| Substantive inference | 32 |
| Mixed | 24 |

This distinction is important because repeating supplied metadata is analytically different from generating an additional social or cultural inference.

---

### Visual-Evidence Annotation

Generated statements are also evaluated according to their relationship with visible evidence in the portrait.

The annotation framework distinguishes:

- visible description;
- visually supported interpretation;
- metadata repetition;
- unsupported social inference;
- incorrect visual hallucination.

The description outputs produced **363 statement-level units**.

A reproducible **25-statement validation subset** was manually reviewed during the pilot to evaluate the feasibility of the annotation procedure.

Because this was a small, single-coder validation exercise, it is **not used to estimate the prevalence of unsupported statements across all 363 statements**.

---

### Semantic Shift

Sentence embeddings and cosine distance are used to quantify semantic differences between country-conditioned descriptions and corresponding control descriptions.

A semantic distance of:

```text
0
```

would indicate highly similar embeddings, while larger values indicate greater semantic divergence.

However, multimodal model outputs can vary even when the image and prompt remain unchanged. Semantic shift is therefore interpreted alongside a same-prompt stochasticity baseline.

---

### Stochasticity Baseline

Repeated generations under the same control prompt were used to estimate normal generation variability.

The pilot produced a mean same-prompt semantic distance of approximately:

```text
D_same = 0.0856
```

Mean country-to-control distances were approximately:

| Condition | Mean semantic distance |
|---|---:|
| Nigeria | 0.1033 |
| India | 0.1117 |
| Saudi Arabia | 0.1118 |
| United Kingdom | 0.1155 |

After paired testing and correction for multiple comparisons, the pilot did not provide evidence that these country-to-control semantic distances systematically exceeded normal same-prompt variability.

Semantic shift is therefore retained as a secondary analytical measure rather than treated as evidence of geographic bias by itself.

---

### Adjective Analysis

Adjectives extracted from the generated descriptions were analysed using a predefined taxonomy covering dimensions such as:

- appearance;
- professionalism/competence;
- warmth;
- modernity/traditionalism;
- socioeconomic framing.

The pilot contained:

- **74 unique observed adjectives**
- **711 adjective tokens**
- **207 taxonomy-assigned tokens**
- **29.1% taxonomy coverage**

The analysis identified descriptive variation across conditions, but none of the tested adjective dimensions remained statistically significant after correction for multiple comparisons.

The limited coverage of the fixed taxonomy is therefore treated as an important methodological limitation.

---

## Statistical Analysis

Because each portrait appears under every experimental condition, the study uses a repeated-measures analytical framework.

The statistical workflow includes:

- aggregation of repeated generations at image level;
- Friedman tests for omnibus repeated-measures comparisons;
- Kendall's W for effect-size estimation;
- Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for paired follow-up comparisons where appropriate;
- correction for multiple comparisons.

The pilot sample is small, so inferential results are interpreted cautiously.

---

## Primary Annotated Analysis

Following image screening, a primary annotated analysis was conducted using the **9 culturally neutral portraits**, with the borderline portrait excluded from the main analysis.

The primary annotated dataset contained **81 statements**.

Four outcomes were evaluated:

- inference presence;
- substantive social/cultural inference;
- metadata repetition;
- visually unsupported statements.

The image-level Friedman tests were:

| Outcome | Friedman χ² | p-value | Kendall's W |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Inference presence | 7.257 | 0.1229 | 0.202 |
| Substantive social/cultural inference | 8.444 | 0.0766 | 0.235 |
| Metadata repetition | 4.836 | 0.3046 | 0.134 |
| Visually unsupported | 5.132 | 0.2740 | 0.143 |

None of these primary annotated outcomes reached conventional statistical significance in the 9-image pilot.

A post-hoc power analysis further indicated that the small primary sample had limited statistical power. These results are therefore interpreted as pilot evidence rather than definitive evidence of either the presence or absence of an effect.

---

## Structured-Output Pilot Analysis

The repository also retains analyses based on the complete **10-image structured-output pilot**.

These analyses examine whether the model populated occupation, personality, and social/cultural inference fields under different experimental conditions.

They are analytically distinct from the manually annotated primary outcomes.

For example, structured personality inference fields appeared more frequently under country-conditioned prompts than under the original control prompt, whereas occupation-field differences were less consistent after corrected pairwise testing.

These results demonstrate changes in **structured inference presence**, not automatically changes in visually unsupported inference.

The distinction between these two analytical layers is maintained throughout the project.

---

## Key Methodological Finding

A central result of the pilot was that several concepts initially treated as similar must be separated:

> **Inference presence ≠ substantive inference ≠ metadata repetition ≠ visually unsupported inference**

A model can produce an inference that is visually plausible.

It can repeat geographic metadata without introducing a new inference.

It can also introduce a substantive social or cultural interpretation without that statement necessarily being demonstrably unsupported by the image.

Separating these concepts substantially improved the methodological defensibility of the study.

---

## Important Limitation of the Original Pilot

An audit of the original experimental prompts found that the control and country-conditioned prompt templates were **not perfectly identical apart from the country metadata**.

The country-conditioned prompts contained additional wording.

Consequently, differences observed in the original pilot cannot be attributed exclusively to the geographic label itself.

The original responses have been retained rather than retrospectively modified or regenerated.

This limitation is documented explicitly and informed the corrected experimental design, in which control and country-conditioned prompts are structurally matched so that geographic metadata can be isolated more cleanly.

## Scope and Interpretation

This repository documents a **pilot study and methodological validation exercise**.

The results should not be interpreted as establishing general behaviour across all multimodal models, geographic contexts, demographic groups, or portrait images.

The pilot uses a small image sample and a single multimodal-model workflow. Its strongest contribution is therefore methodological: demonstrating how geographic-context effects can be investigated while distinguishing structured inference presence, metadata repetition, substantive inference, stochastic variation, and visual support.

The project also demonstrates the importance of auditing prompt equivalence, defining the independent experimental unit correctly, and validating apparently "unsupported" model claims against the source image before drawing conclusions about multimodal AI bias.
